# Rae Lovejoy

**Systems thinker, builder, writer, and compulsive connector of things.**

I’m interested in the structures underneath everyday life: infrastructure, institutions, technology, power, memory, community, and the strange ways they all shape one another.

A lot of my work asks some version of:

> **What changes when we stop treating the current arrangement as inevitable?**

I build software and infrastructure, write speculative and analytical work, run self-hosted systems, and turn questions into projects until they occasionally become entire ecosystems.

## Current work

- **[Root Sequence](https://github.com/Root-Sequence)** — a living ecosystem for systems research, exploratory writing, frameworks, practical experiments, speculative worlds, and tools.
- **Community Infrastructure** — community-controlled infrastructure for helping people find community, communicate, coordinate, share resources, preserve knowledge, govern themselves, and remain resilient without surrendering control to a centralized platform.
- **[Museum of Ordinary Life](https://github.com/Museum-of-Ordinary-Life)** — a living, participatory archive documenting ordinary human life while it is still ordinary.
- **[No One Noticed](https://noonenoticed.world/)** — speculative fiction and systems exploration about an invisible singularity, ordinary continuity, freedom, consent, legitimacy, maintenance, and what a more coherent world might actually feel like.
- **[Being Human(e)](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/beinghumane-guide)** — an evolving field guide about consequences, responsibility, care, power, accessibility, and what it means to build and live more humanely.

One shorthand for how some of these projects relate:

> **Root Sequence discovers. Community Infrastructure tests. Coherent World designs. No One Noticed experiences. The Museum of Ordinary Life preserves.**

## Things I keep coming back to

- open-source and self-hosted infrastructure;
- Linux, networking, homelabs, automation, and local AI;
- privacy, consent, provenance, accessibility, and community control;
- local-first, resilient, repairable, replaceable systems;
- archives, memory, ordinary life, and the invisible systems beneath it;
- emergence, feedback loops, power, institutions, and unintended consequences;
- speculative futures grounded in real history, technology, and human possibility.

## How I like to build

I’m generally more interested in **capacity than engagement, stewardship than ownership, interoperability than lock-in, and evidence than hype**.

Some recurring rules:

- prototypes are evidence, not destiny;
- automation should reduce burden without hiding responsibility;
- important decisions should remain legible;
- documentation should stay coherent without duplicating everything everywhere;
- infrastructure should be understandable, repairable, exportable, and replaceable where possible;
- a technically elegant system that people cannot safely use, maintain, leave, or govern is not actually elegant.
